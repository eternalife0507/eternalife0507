SummaryWhat the synthetic study says to keep, change and add

17 recommendations from the synthesis, 10 of them high confidence. “High” means the signal was consistent across roles and both markets in this simulation. It does not mean validated. Every row needs confirmation in real interviews (Step 21), using the field guide.

Keep

  • The problem framing. Signals stay verbal (KA3, 10/10), nobody owns cross-contract closure (KA10, 9/10), lag and mis-routing (KA5, 7/10).
  • Flat project pricing with no per-seat charge (KA11, 6/6 buyers).
  • The pilot’s “publish the miss” discipline and its week-12 retention floor.

Change

  • Price. 0 of 6 accept $150k as-is, and $24k reads as “the craft aren’t covered.” The median WTP point is $95k.
  • Lead value. Lead with verified cross-employer closure and triage hours, not stoppage or LD avoidance (KA9, 50%).
  • The AI story. “A person always reviews” is table stakes (KA7, 0/10 fully validated). The reviewer is a persona.

Add

  • Vetoes to the DMU: labor, legal, IT security, procurement, owner contracts (10/10 named one).
  • A reporter-identity model and no-login reporting (8/10 and 9/10).
  • Four new key assumptions: anonymity, reviewer capacity, legality of an owner-held queue, and the purchasing unit.

Step 6 — Full Life Cycle Use CaseHigh confidence
Deck today

Report by voice, photo or text in your own language; Ask for the applicable rule; Recognize a peer. Slides 4–5.

Revise to

Design for reporting at break with accurate location recall (phones banned at many work faces), a morning pre-task or TBM open-items feed, and night-shift and turnover handoff.

Step 7 — High-Level Product SpecHigh confidence
Deck today

Report, Ask (cited IOGP, client rules, KOSHA, OSH Act), Recognize (points exchange, misuse limits). “AI is labeled. A person always reviews.” Slides 4–5.

Revise to

Make four things spec-level: (1) independently verified closure with time- and location-bound evidence plus interim-measure status; (2) a reporter-identity model hidden from employers by default, visible only to the reviewer, with a follow-up channel; (3) no install and no login (QR, SMS or kiosk); (4) push to Procore, enterprise EHS, e-permit or HQ portal. Ship 'what's the rule here' disabled or restricted to controlled documents, and have it refuse permit questions.

Synthetic evidence: Unmet need 10/10; identity 8/10; device access 9/10; another-inbox 7/10; rule-citation risk 6/10.

Step 8 — Quantified Value PropositionHigh confidence
Deck today

“Safety is the shortest lever on the schedule.” Fewer stoppages and schedule certainty. Pilot floors: W12 retention ≥40%, acceptance ≥95%, closure ≥60% in 14 days. Slides 3, 7, 10.

Revise to

Lead with measurable hours and days: triage and reconciliation hours saved, and cross-employer time to verified close (e.g., Marcus: 11 days to under 3). Treat stoppage and LD avoidance as a year-long, jointly built baseline, not a 12-week claim.

Synthetic evidence: Triage figures from Carlos, Park, Marcus, Bob (4/6); KA9 50% V; Marcus and Bob said 12 weeks can't show stoppage reduction.
